12. When an attention mask changes an answer

The previous chapters found two different failures: a model can assert a fact that the text never supplies, and a rule can look perfect on familiar wording before failing on a new author’s examples. I tried a narrower zero-shot intervention before training another head. A frozen reranker selected two short ranges from each passage. I left each decision model’s weights and question unchanged, then altered which source tokens its attention could use. If the answer changed, I could at least locate an inference-time lever. A changed answer alone would not show that the model had found the right evidence. Evidence E11

The test used eighteen authored diagnostic families: people management, visa sponsorship and employment type, each under explicit support, explicit denial, silence, uncertainty or hypothetical wording, conflict, and a wrong-scope or quoted mention. One extended passage per family was used for the mask comparison. The references were independently model-reviewed proposals, not human gold. Twelve of the eighteen references were unknown, so a constant-unknown answer already scores 12/18 while recognizing none of the six stated facts. The numbers below describe this inspected fixture; they do not estimate performance on job postings.

Diagram: A single routing tactic can repair a negation and a conflict while creating an unsupported assertion elsewhere.

The first flip is unusually concrete. In the fictional Data Analyst example, the source says the hire “will not manage employees as direct reports.” Laya base called the positive management claim supported before masking and denied after it. Lev called it supported before its soft bias and merely unresolved afterward: a safer answer, but still not the explicit denial the passage supplies. In a second example, HR confirmed sponsorship while the hiring lead denied it for the same opening. Laya changed supported to the reference unresolved under the hard mask. These are answer changes on extended passages that also contain irrelevant career-glossary and company text; the quoted sentences are excerpts, not the whole input.

The regression matters just as much. A recruiter explicitly did not know whether the Data Analyst role was permanent or contracting. Laya base correctly returned unresolved without a mask, then asserted supported with it. The management hypothetical and a company-wide management policy also remained wrongly substantive under Laya’s mask. HRM-Text returned wrong labels on both. For Lev, the soft bias corrected two uncertain cases by changing supported to unresolved, but added no support or denial recognition. Qwen’s apparently respectable 12/18 came from answering unknown to every case; it missed all six explicit support/denial cases.

The broader HRM-Text run helps test a tempting explanation. Across 144 dependent rows, it recovered 20/24 supports and 24/24 denials, but only 21/96 unknowns. It failed all 24 conflict variants and 23/24 wrong-scope variants. In 72 clean-versus-keyword-distractor pairs, 14 predictions changed, yet correctness was 32 without the distractor and 33 with it. Keyword distraction affected answers, but did not produce a consistent net loss. “It is only matching keywords” is not established by these data. Relation, subject and source binding remain plausible problems.

Diagram: The intervention establishes a causal effect on this model’s output, not a unique causal explanation for its improved score.

The identity-mask checks protect the comparison against a broken adapter: Laya’s base and typed heads reproduced their no-mask raw logits on all 36 checkpoint-family pairs; Lev did so on all eighteen. The frozen relevance reranker retained the complete original evidence string in only 13/18 cases at the narrowest no-overlap setting, and a lexical selector tied its source-retention counts in every chunk cell. Moreover, source-string retention does not prove the decisive clause survived. Some Laya corrections occurred even when the selected ranges omitted part of that original string. The selected text may be changing the model’s general willingness to abstain rather than showing it the right clause.

My next causal test is therefore not “try another model.” Hold the question, readout, selected-token budget and fresh grouped examples fixed. Compare the learned reranker with lexical, prefix-only, random and an explicitly annotated clause oracle; move the decisive clause to the beginning, middle and end. Add clause deletion, negation and wrong-subject edits. If oracle selection works but the learned selector fails, retrieval is the bottleneck. If every short mask helps, compression or answer bias is more likely. If no mask works, the decision interface is the likelier seam. If learned selection uniquely wins on independently authored groups, only then can I credit relevance routing. This test has not run. None of these diagnostic outputs enters the active workflow.
